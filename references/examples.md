# Entirely fictional worked examples

Created for this version from generic service operations. These are teaching illustrations,
not historical cases or model test outputs. No example represents a private client or a career event.

## Vague complaints about a repair booking service

“We are getting bad reviews” does not tell us whether the cause is missed visits, workmanship,
billing or hostile misinformation. Ask which promised service failed and whether people are
currently waiting; the answer changes the immediate remedy. Offer to acknowledge an unresolved
complaint now. Once missed appointments and dispatch capacity are known, prioritize contacting
waiting customers, reducing new bookings and a realistic rescheduling/refund choice. A three-person
shop can assign its owner dispatch and refund authority, one colleague customer contact and one
delivery; it does not need a multinational crisis committee. Stop asking when the user needs
today's workable schedule; mark remaining root-cause checks beside tomorrow's improvement.

## A planetarium with an unfinished sound review

An internal technician's limited check is usable in planning but is not an independent safety
clearance. Options can include quieter sessions with monitoring, a partial pause for the disputed
program, or a full pause if the possible harm justifies it. Explain operational costs, visitor
needs and the evidence that distinguishes the options. A statement may describe actions and the
scope of the check without declaring all sessions safe. If a later reading falls outside the
technician's reviewed range, retract the premise of adequate coverage and reassess the affected
program. Do not continue reassurance on the old basis.

## Double-booked workspace, different resources

For a small workspace: the owner decides refunds within a stated cap, staff contact each affected
customer, and one shared booking register prevents another collision. Offer replacement space
only after confirming availability and accessibility. For a network: appoint an incident owner,
give local managers defined remedy authority, require platform operations to fix synchronization,
and have headquarters approve shared factual claims and exceptional expenditure. These are
different implementations of the same duty to restore promised access.

## Bilingual service outage

A preliminary operations check showing intact stored files is not a finding that access permissions
were unaffected. Chinese and English updates must agree on affected functions, evidence scope,
what users should do and the next update. If a new permissions fault is confirmed, withdraw any
broader reassurance, restrict affected access and have the competent security/privacy owner assess
scope and obligations. Translation must not strengthen “no loss found in checked storage” into
“no data was affected.”

## Approval and bounded commitments

Keep proposal, approval and execution separate. Name the approving role and requested resource.
An approved spending ceiling does not authorize every purpose. If approval is missing, offer a
feasible interim service option without promising compensation, new spending or warranties.
Replace guarantees of permanent prevention with a concrete fix, a check date, an accountable owner
and a trigger for revisiting the decision. Keep this distinction in every language.
