# From consultation to action / 完整顾问交付

Read when converging on an integrated plan, after the immediate diagnosis and decisive unknowns
have been addressed or explicitly bounded. A sufficiently detailed opening can justify this in
the first turn, after the decision guide. Reuse the file later; it is not the initial intake.

Use these five checks internally, showing only what helps this user act. A two-person business
may need a short action note. Alongside a conditional plan, still ask the user directly about a
missing fact that would change the recommendation. With sufficient information, proceed without
extra questions; an explicit request for no more questions calls for clear assumptions instead.

1. **Decision and basis.** Retain the agreed outcome, current diagnosis, confirmed facts, internal
   judgments and assumptions. Explain the recommendation and strongest feasible alternative,
   including opportunity, downside, evidence and what would change the choice.
2. **Practical resolution.** Correct the immediate service/product/process problem and make relief
   accessible. Check that the affected person actually receives it; a notice sent or refund
   submitted is not completion. Separate today's case from optional longer-term mechanisms.
3. **People, authority and resources.** Assign realistic decision, action and checking roles using
   known capacity. Combine functions for small teams; specify headquarters/local powers where
   needed. Apply the working basis in SKILL.md to scope the resource request and decision owner;
   for a gap, give a proposed checkpoint and feasible interim action.
4. **Audiences and communication.** Decide who needs to know first and whether targeted contact or
   public communication is warranted. Match message, service channel, spokesperson and approval
   to the actual audience. Identify any apology, liability or reporting question requiring local
   qualified review. Keep bilingual facts and commitments equivalent; prepare media or frontline
   answers only as useful for this plan.
   Follow the [source → update → current delivery sequence](../SKILL.md#one-basis-for-action-and-copy):
   for each needed audience-facing deliverable, provide one currently usable version, keeping
   pending options in internal advice unless alternative drafts are requested. Reconcile each
   version's material claims, promises and action order with the current plan and original
   sources before presenting it; a correct analysis alone is not enough.
5. **Outcomes and revision.** Use a few observable outcomes with an owner and checkpoint, rather
   than reach alone. State which new facts or failures change the decision, who updates affected
   people, and which earlier statement needs correction. End with the next executable action.

Use plain language and necessary sources for consequential facts, changing rules or prices. Keep
dates, budgets and roles consistent with the working basis. A historical analogy is
an aid to judgment, not current evidence or qualified clearance. Promise checks and corrective
actions within authority, rather than guaranteed trust, zero risk or permanent prevention.

## Miniature: enough information to finish the plan

**User:** I own a two-person translation studio in Canada. From 1 May, new orders will take ten
business days. Existing orders keep their agreed dates. My colleague confirmed that capacity.
I approve the website update and client notice; I will send it, and my colleague handles the site
and quotations. We want to retain clients without promising faster delivery. No advertising budget.
Please give us a practical plan and an English notice now.

**Adviser:** The risk is confusing new turnaround times with existing commitments. Publish the change
before 1 May: your colleague updates the website and quotation replies together, and you send the
notice through your existing client channel. Your brief supplies the capacity and approval, so no
further intake is needed. Keep current orders on their agreed dates. Use this client notice:

> From 1 May, our turnaround for new translation orders will be ten business days. Dates already
> agreed for existing orders remain unchanged. Please reply with your project requirements when
> requesting a quotation so we can confirm the delivery date for that order.

Use the existing two roles and channels, with no paid campaign. Check client questions and delivery
performance together after the first week. Offering a turnaround shorter than the
confirmed ten business days might appeal to urgent enquiries, but is not supported by the stated
capacity; the stated turnaround is the practical choice. If capacity falls,
limit acceptance of new work and revise future quotations rather than silently change agreed dates.
The next step is the coordinated site/quotation update and notice, followed by checking that clients
understand which orders are affected.
