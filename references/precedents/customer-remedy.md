# Customer remedies: promise, system, receipt

Researcher initial review, 2026-11-17; not independent audit or legal advice. Historical observations
below are bounded to the cited sources. The decision rules are this project's synthesis. Recheck
later developments before treating a historical position as current. No claim of measured PR causality.

## P01 Qantas: a cancellation must reach every customer-facing system

**Observed:** The ACCC reported on 8 October 2024 that the Federal Court ordered a A$100 million
penalty after Qantas admitted misleading consumers through sales and delayed notices for flights
already cancelled internally. A separate undertaking provided about A$20 million in additional
customer payments; this does not establish that every eligible person received money. The ACCC
also reported operating/scheduling changes.
[ACCC court outcome](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/federal-court-orders-qantas-to-pay-100m-in-penalties-for-misleading-consumers).

**Apply when:** An internal decision and booking, billing or customer notices disagree. Ask which
system is authoritative, when downstream systems update, and who can stop further affected sales.
Operations/product own reconciliation, service owns individual options, finance owns payment status;
leadership resolves the dependency. For a small operator, one owner and a second-person check suffice.
**Failure boundary:** An apology or changed website does not clear stale bookings or indirect channels.
Do not copy airline compensation amounts into another business, or infer the operational fix restored trust.

## P02 Airbnb Australia: correct the transaction, not only the wording

**Observed:** The ACCC's 20 December 2023 report describes a A$15 million penalty for misleading
currency presentation, and an undertaking offering up to A$15 million compensation. USD labels
had been introduced in August 2021. Some complaints had wrongly been attributed to users' own
currency selection. Compensation design included exchange-rate differences and foreign transaction fees.
[ACCC currency case](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/airbnb-to-pay-15m-in-penalties-and-up-to-15m-compensation-for-misleading-consumers).

**Apply when:** A displayed price and actual charge differ. Compare screenshots, locale settings,
transaction records and fees before blaming users. Product fixes display/consent, finance scopes
individual loss, support gives a secure claim route and an escalation route for missing records.
**Failure boundary:** Better labels do not resolve prior charges. A proposed scheme is not completed
redress; a global company must check the contracting entity and local market separately.

## P03 Wowcher: consent and access are part of the remedy

**Observed:** In July 2024 Wowcher signed undertakings covering accurate urgency/scarcity claims,
ending pre-ticked VIP sign-ups and refunding affected membership fees in credit with a cash-conversion
option, with reporting to the CMA. These are commitments, not evidence of each refund being received.
[CMA case and dated updates](https://www.gov.uk/cma-cases/wowcher-group-consumer-protection-case).

**Apply when:** A default enrolment or misleading timer drives complaints. Preserve consent records,
stop the disputed mechanism, identify affected cohorts and make cancellation/redress usable.
Commerce owns the interface, finance the payment route and an accountable manager checks completion.
**Failure boundary:** Credit is not universally an acceptable substitute for cash; a notice that users
cannot act on is not redress. Check actual rights and constraints before copying this case's scheme.

## Compare before choosing

P01 concerns a decision-to-system gap; P02 concerns price meaning and actual financial loss; P03
concerns choice architecture and consent. Similar complaints need different repairs. First locate
where the customer journey fails, then choose the smallest remedy that addresses it. Check direct
customer access and received outcomes before announcing success. None of these sources isolates
communications as the cause of commercial recovery, and none licenses blanket refunds without authority.
