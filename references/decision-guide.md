# Decisions under incomplete information

The following consultation rules are project designs, not a validated numerical scoring model.

Read once at the start of a new consultation when file access is available. The core loop in
SKILL.md remains usable if this file cannot be loaded. Reuse the guide across later turns.

## Choosing the next decision and question

Start with the user's chosen outcome, a tentative explanation of the current obstacle and one
useful action supported by what is already known. Offer a different objective as a choice rather
than silently changing the task. A formal-looking prompt is not proof that the underlying facts
are complete; a detailed factual brief may need no questions at all.

Select the distinction that changes protection, remedy, a public claim, authority or timing.
For an evaluation of a specific object, obtain its decisive wording or current arrangement first.
Ask one or two concise questions when needed, accepting a short example instead of a dossier.
A count is useful early only when it affects urgency or capacity. Link each question to its action
consequence; avoid bundling a long intake list inside one sentence.

After an answer, retire resolved questions and revise the working premise. If a report or fact is
unavailable, narrow the claim or offer bounded branches. A request for a plan means provide useful
advice now, not automatically suppress the one distinction that could change it. If the user wants
no further questions, show the assumption and its consequence instead. Stop when enough is known
for the next decision; a later discovery can reopen that decision without repeating the intake.

## Evidence and public use

| Intended use | What may be adequate | What would change the recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reversible internal planning | Credible attributed internal assessment with scope and uncertainty | Contradictory observations, limited coverage, changed conditions |
| Limited external explanation | Checkable observation or attributed preliminary finding, accurately bounded and useful to the audience | Wording invites reliance beyond the finding; serious unassessed harm; relevant contrary evidence |
| Formal assurance or definitive causal claim | Evidence sufficient for that exact claim, competent review and applicable obligations | Missing material tests, unresolved contradiction, a different population or time period |

These are not three automatic approval stamps. Match the evidence threshold to the decision,
stakes and audience reliance. Formal publication does not always require an independent report;
some claims can be established by reliable first-party records. A high-stakes assurance may need
stronger evidence than a disclaimer can provide. Identify what the missing evidence would settle.

When an internal assessment is credible, reason from it conditionally. Compare a bounded service
continuation with a partial or full pause if both are plausible: benefits, residual exposure,
resource cost, reversibility, evidence needed and a clear switch trigger. Explain the choice.

## Scale the organization

If team capacity or decision rights determine feasibility, ask before scheduling the work.
When they remain unknown, propose only the minimum work for the current case and put broader
mechanisms in a later phase, without inventing staff or hours. For a small team, name a decision owner and combine operational repair, customer support and
communication roles where realistic. Specify a second check for consequential claims or refunds;
use an available colleague, owner review or appropriate external specialist rather than inventing
departments. If no second person is available, narrow and defer the unsupported commitment.

For a large organization, specify headquarters' authority over common facts, total budget and
material commitments; local authority over delivery, language and service channels within agreed
bounds; the escalation threshold and expected response time. Operational leaders verify the facts,
communications drafts, the authorized decision owner approves. Local obligations may require
local professional review; do not assume headquarters approval overrides them.

## Minimum executable decision

Use prose or a compact table only as helpful. Identify who may decide and fund the response,
who fixes the root problem, what relief affected people get, who verifies the claim, who approves
and speaks, when the next checkpoint occurs, and how outcomes will be observed. Explicitly label
proposed deadlines and budget assumptions. A plan without authority or capacity is not executable.

On change, state: prior premise → new evidence → actions stopped or revised → actions retained →
next checkpoint. Protect the record of earlier conclusions rather than silently rewriting it.

## Approval and bounded commitments

Use the single working-basis mechanism in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md#one-basis-for-action-and-copy)
for authority and commitments. If a decision is pending, offer a feasible interim service option,
identify its trade-off and the specific decision that would enable the fuller remedy.
Replace guarantees of permanent prevention with a concrete fix, a check date, an accountable owner
and a trigger for revisiting the decision. Keep this distinction in every language.
