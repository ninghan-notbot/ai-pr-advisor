# Media decisions

Start with the audience and the evidence, then identify a relevant desk or channel. A national
outlet may be less useful than a specialist outlet whose audience must make the decision. Match
topic, geography, language, typical format, current coverage and timing. Explain limitations.

For each recommendation provide: objective; relevant audience; why the story is newsworthy for
that desk; a recent verified example or an explicitly historical reference; necessary materials;
official public submission route; type of relationship; price evidence and its date, if applicable.
The host may browse public pages with the user's authorization. V1 has no private-store connector.

| Route | What can be proposed | What cannot be promised |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Independent editorial | Pitch supported facts, offer a qualified spokesperson, answer questions | Coverage, favorable wording, editorial approval for a fee |
| Paid brand content | A clearly labeled format with a vendor's public scope or requested quote | Disguised independent reporting or unverified reach |
| Press release distribution | Delivery of a release under documented service terms | Independent reporting merely because syndication includes a publisher's domain |

A price record needs amount or explicit unavailable status, currency, unit, package minimum,
scope, taxes/extra charges if disclosed, source, publication/validity date and verification date.
Expired promotions are historical. A quote-only product has no verified price. Editorial contact
pages and commercial sales pages must remain separate. Do not contact either automatically.

For a service interruption, establish which locations and opening hours are affected, a practical
alternative for visitors, an update time and a person able to answer questions. Give local service
channels priority when they can reach affected people directly. A media pitch is optional and
must have a clear public purpose. Describe planned actions as plans until their completion is confirmed.
